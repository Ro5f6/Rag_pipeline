Embedding Models and Vector Representations

An embedding model maps a piece of text to a fixed-length vector of floating point numbers, positioned so that texts with similar meanings land near each other. This is what makes semantic search possible: a query about "reducing cloud spend" can retrieve a passage about "lowering infrastructure costs" despite sharing no words at all.

Modern text embedding models are transformer encoders trained with contrastive objectives. The model sees pairs of related and unrelated texts and learns to pull related pairs together in vector space while pushing unrelated pairs apart. Sentence-transformers models such as all-MiniLM-L6-v2 are trained this way on over a billion sentence pairs.

Dimensionality is the length of the output vector. all-MiniLM-L6-v2 produces 384 dimensions, while larger models such as bge-large and e5-large produce 1024. Higher dimensionality can capture more nuance but costs proportionally more memory and search time: a million vectors at 384 dimensions occupies roughly 1.5 GB in float32, and 4 GB at 1024 dimensions.

Normalization matters for search efficiency. If every vector is scaled to unit length, the dot product between two vectors equals their cosine similarity. This lets a vector index use a plain inner-product search rather than computing cosine similarity with a division on every comparison.

Model choice involves a three-way tradeoff between quality, latency, and cost. MiniLM-class models run comfortably on CPU at a few milliseconds per passage and are strong enough for most prototypes. Larger open models improve recall on technical and domain-specific corpora. Hosted embedding APIs remove the operational burden but add network latency to every ingest and every query, and they bill per token.

A critical operational constraint is that the same model must embed both the corpus and the queries. Vectors from different models are not comparable, so changing the embedding model requires re-embedding the entire corpus from scratch. Treat the embedding model as part of the index's identity, not as a swappable runtime setting.
